Present Tense · A1

Presente de Indicativo

A1

"The present tense" — what you do, what's happening now, general truths

The Core Rule

The present tense — what you do, what's happening now, general truths. Remove the infinitive ending (-ar, -er, -ir) and add the corresponding person endings. This is the first tense you learn and the most commonly used in everyday Spanish.

Essential Distinction · A1

Ser vs Estar

A1

"Both mean 'to be' but are used differently" — ser for identity/essence, estar for states/location

The Core Rule

Both mean "to be" but are used differently — ser for identity/essence, estar for states/location. Mixing them up changes meaning entirely: Es aburrido (He's boring) vs. Está aburrido (He's bored).

Ser (identity / essence)

nosotrossomos
vosotrossois
ellosson
Estar (states / location)
yoestoy
túestás
él/ellaestá
nosotrosestamos
vosotrosestáis
ellosestán
🪪
SER — Identity
Soy estudiante de español.
I am a Spanish student.
🌎
SER — Origin
Ella es de Colombia.
She is from Colombia.
✨
SER — Characteristics
Mi hermano es alto y simpático.
My brother is tall and nice.
📍
ESTAR — Location
Estoy en la biblioteca.
I am in the library.
😊
ESTAR — Emotions/states
Hoy estoy muy cansado.
Today I am very tired.
🌡️
ESTAR — Temporary conditions
La sopa está caliente.
The soup is hot.
Mnemonic

SER = DOCTOR (Description, Occupation, Characteristic, Time, Origin, Relationship) / ESTAR = PLACE (Position, Location, Action, Condition, Emotion)

Stem-Changing Verbs (Present)

A1

"Boot verbs" — the stem vowel changes for all forms EXCEPT nosotros and vosotros

The Core Rule

Some verbs change their stem vowel in the present tense for all forms EXCEPT nosotros and vosotros. The three patterns are: e→ie, o→ue, and e→i. The pattern of affected forms looks like a boot shape in the conjugation table — hence "boot verbs."

ellospueden
e→i: pedir (to ask for)

Verb Types · A2

Verbos Reflexivos

A2

"Reflexive verbs" — the action is done to oneself

The Core Rule

Reflexive verbs show the action is done to oneself — the subject and object are the same person. They are identified by the -se ending on the infinitive (levantarse, ducharse). The reflexive pronoun (me, te, se, nos, os, se) goes before the conjugated verb.

levantarse (to get up)

túte levantas
él/ellase levanta
nosotrosnos levantamos
vosotrosos levantáis
ellosse levantan

Near Future · A2

Ir + a + Infinitivo

A2

"Going to do" — the near future, plans, and intentions

The Core Rule

The near future — what you're going to do. Use ir (conjugated) + a + infinitive verb. This is the most common way to express future plans in spoken Spanish, even more than the Futuro Simple.

ir (conjugated) + a + infinitive
yovoy a hablar
túvas a comer
él/ellava a vivir
nosotrosvamos a estudiar
vosotrosvais a leer
ellosvan a escribir
Trigger Words → use Ir + a + Infinitivo

📋
Plans

✈️
Intentions

We're going to travel to Mexico next year.
🌧️

Verb Patterns · A2

Gustar y Similares

A2

"To like" — but backwards! The thing liked is the subject

The Core Rule

Gustar doesn't work like "to like" — the thing liked is the subject. Use me/te/le/nos/os/les + gusta (singular) or gustan (plural). Literally, "coffee is pleasing to me" not "I like coffee."

gustar — singular (gusta)

(a ti)te gusta
(a él/ella/Ud.)le gusta
(a nosotros)nos gusta
(a vosotros)os gusta
(a ellos/Uds.)les gusta
gustar — plural (gustan)
(a mí)me gustan
(a ti)te gustan
(a él/ella/Ud.)le gustan
(a nosotros)nos gustan
(a vosotros)os gustan
(a ellos/Uds.)les gustan
Similar Verbs (same pattern as gustar)
encantarmolestar interesarimportar fascinarpreocupar parecerfaltar dolerquedar
☕
Singular — one thing

I like coffee.
📚
Plural — multiple things

I like history books.
✈️
Infinitive — an activity

🎵
Encantar — to love (stronger)

Past Tense · B1

Pretérito Indefinido

B1

"The simple past" — something happened and it's done

The Core Rule

Use this for completed actions at a specific point in time. The action started AND finished in the past. It doesn't matter if it took 5 seconds or 5 years — if it's over and you're thinking of it as a single finished event, use Indefinido.

túhablaste
él/ellahabló
nosotroshablamos
vosotroshablasteis
elloshablaron

yocomí / viví
túcomiste / viviste
él/ellacomió / vivió
nosotroscomimos / vivimos
vosotroscomisteis / vivisteis
elloscomieron / vivieron

Past Tense · B1

Pretérito Imperfecto

B1

"The background past" — what was ongoing, habitual, or how things were

The Core Rule

Use this for ongoing states, descriptions, and habitual/repeated actions in the past — things that don't have a clear beginning or end, or that happened "used to." Think of it as the background "film" that the Indefinido's specific events interrupt.

-AR verbs → hablar
yohablaba
túhablabas
él/ellahablaba
nosotroshablábamos
vosotroshablabais
elloshablaban
-ER/-IR verbs → comer / vivir
yocomía / vivía
túcomías / vivías
él/ellacomía / vivía
nosotroscomíamos / vivíamos
vosotroscomíais / vivíais
elloscomían / vivían
Good News
Only 3 irregular verbs in the Imperfecto: ser (era/eras/era/éramos/erais/eran), ir (iba/ibas/iba/íbamos/ibais/iban), ver (veía/veías...). Everything else is perfectly regular!
Trigger Words → use Imperfecto
siemprenunca normalmentegeneralmente a vecestodos los días de niño/acuando era joven antesen aquella época mientrascada semana
🔁
Habitual/repeated actions (used to)
De niño, siempre jugaba en el parque.
As a child, I always used to play in the park.
🎨
Describing background conditions / states
El restaurante era pequeño y hacía mucho calor.
The restaurant was small and it was very hot.
⏳
Action in progress when something else happened
Dormía cuando llegó mi hermano.
I was sleeping when my brother arrived. (Dormía = ongoing, llegó = interruption)
🕐
Telling the time or age in the past
Eran las tres de la tarde. Tenía doce años.
It was three in the afternoon. I was twelve years old.
Critical Contrast · B1

Indefinido vs. Imperfecto

The most important distinction in B1 Spanish

The Film Analogy

Think of a film scene: the Imperfecto is the background scene that was already "playing" — the weather, the mood, what people were doing. The Indefinido is the specific event that happens in that scene — the shot that fires, the door that slams.

Side-by-Side Contrasts
Imperfecto (background/habit)
Indefinido (event/completion)
Vivía en Madrid.I used to live / I was living in Madrid. (no defined end)
Viví en Madrid tres años.I lived in Madrid for three years. (defined, finished period)
Quería ser médico.I wanted to be a doctor. (ongoing desire in past)
Quiso hablar con ella.He tried to speak with her. (specific attempt)
Conocía a tu hermano.I knew your brother. (had prior knowledge)
Conocí a tu hermano en la fiesta.I met your brother at the party. (first encounter)
No podía dormir.I couldn't sleep (general state).
No pude dormir.I couldn't sleep (and failed — that night specifically).
Verb Meaning Changes
Saber, conocer, poder, querer, and tener change meaning in Indefinido vs Imperfecto! Sabía = I knew. Supe = I found out. Tenía = I had. Tuve = I received/got.
Past Tense · B1

Pretérito Perfecto

B1

"Have done" — recent past still connected to now

The Core Rule

Formed with haber + participio. Use it when the past action is connected to or relevant to the present moment — either it happened today/this week/this year, or its effects are still felt now. In Spain it overlaps heavily with Indefinido for recent events; Latin America mostly uses Indefinido instead.

Formula: haber (present) + participio
yohe hablado
túhas hablado
él/ellaha hablado
nosotroshemos hablado
vosotroshabéis hablado
elloshan hablado
Irregular Participios (key ones)
abrirabierto
decirdicho
escribirescrito
hacerhecho
morirmuerto
ponerpuesto
romperroto
vervisto
volvervuelto
Trigger Words → use Perfecto
hoyesta mañana/tarde esta semanaeste mes/año yatodavía no alguna veznunca últimamenterecientemente
📅
Actions within a time frame still in progress
Esta semana he trabajado mucho.
This week I have worked a lot. (the week isn't over)
🌍
Life experiences (ever/never)
¿Has visitado alguna vez Japón?
Have you ever visited Japan?
🔗
Past action with present consequence
He perdido las llaves. (so I can't get in now)
I have lost my keys.
Spain vs Latin America
In Spain: "Esta mañana he comido bien." In most of Latin America: "Esta mañana comí bien." Both are correct — Latin America prefers Indefinido even for recent events.
Future Tense · B1

Futuro Simple

B1

"Will do" — also expresses probability/supposition

The Core Rule

Add endings directly to the infinitive (no stem changes for regular verbs). Used for future plans, predictions, and — very importantly at B2 — expressing probability in the present tense ("I wonder if...", "It must be...").

Regular: add to infinitive
yohablaré
túhablarás
él/ellahablará
nosotroshablaremos
vosotroshablaréis
elloshablarán
Irregular stems (same endings apply)
tenertendr-
venirvendr-
ponerpondr-
salirsaldr-
hacerhar-
decirdir-
poderpodr-
sabersabr-
haberhabr-
quererquerr-
📅
Future plans or predictions
El próximo año viviremos en París.
Next year we will live in Paris.
🤔
Probability / speculation about the present (B2!)
¿Dónde estará Juan? — Estará en el trabajo.
Where could Juan be? — He's probably at work.
⚠️
Note: ir a + infinitive is more common in speech
Voy a llamarte mañana. (more natural in conversation)
I'm going to call you tomorrow.
Conditional · B1/B2

Condicional Simple

B1/B2

"Would do" — hypotheticals, politeness, and reported speech

The Core Rule

Same irregular stems as Futuro Simple, but different endings. Three main uses: (1) hypothetical consequences ("would"), (2) polite requests, and (3) reported speech (what someone said they would do).

Regular: infinitive + endings
yohablaría
túhablarías
él/ellahablaría
nosotroshablaríamos
vosotroshablaríais
elloshablarían
Irregular stems (same as Futuro!)
tenertendría
hacerharía
decirdiría
poderpodría
quererquerría
sabersabría
venirvendría
salirsaldría
💡
Hypothetical consequences (the "then" in if/then)
Si tuviera dinero, compraría una casa.
If I had money, I would buy a house.
🤝
Polite requests (softer than present tense)
¿Podrías ayudarme? / Me gustaría reservar una mesa.
Could you help me? / I would like to reserve a table.
💬
Reported speech (what someone said they "would" do)
Dijo que vendría a las ocho.
He said he would come at eight.
🤔
Probability in the past (B2)
Serían las tres cuando llegó.
It was probably three o'clock when he arrived.
Subjunctive · B1/B2

Subjuntivo Presente

B1/B2

"The mood of uncertainty" — wishes, doubts, emotions, and future time clauses

How to Form It

Take the yo form of the present indicative, drop the -o, and add opposite vowel endings: -AR verbs get -E endings; -ER/-IR verbs get -A endings. "Opposite vowel" is the key trick.

-AR verb → hablar (yo: hablo → habl-)
yohable
túhables
él/ellahable
nosotroshablemos
vosotroshabléis
elloshablen
-ER/-IR verb → comer / vivir
yocoma / viva
túcomas / vivas
él/ellacoma / viva
nosotroscomamos / vivamos
vosotroscomáis / viváis
elloscoman / vivan
Key Irregulars in Subjuntivo Presente
ser → sea, seas, sea… | estar → esté, estés… | ir → vaya, vayas… | haber → haya | saber → sepa | dar → dé | tener → tenga (follows yo form rule: tengo → teng-)
🙏
Wishes and desires (querer que, desear que, esperar que)
Quiero que vengas conmigo.
I want you to come with me. (two different subjects = subjunctive)
😊
Emotions about others' actions (alegrarse de que, temer que)
Me alegra que estés bien.
I'm glad that you're well.
🤨
Doubt or denial (no creer que, dudar que)
No creo que tenga razón.
I don't think she's right.
⚖️
Recommendations and necessity (es necesario que, es mejor que)
Es importante que llegues a tiempo.
It's important that you arrive on time.
🔮
Future time clauses (cuando, en cuanto, hasta que, antes de que)
Te llamo cuando llegue a casa.
I'll call you when I get home. (not "cuando llego" — it's in the future!)
🔍
Indefinite/hypothetical antecedent
Busco un piso que tenga dos baños. (vs. "que tiene" — I know which one)
I'm looking for a flat that has two bathrooms (any one that does).
Past Perfect · B2

Pretérito Pluscuamperfecto

B2

"Had done" — the past before the past

The Core Rule

Formed with haber (imperfecto) + participio. Use when you're already talking about the past and need to refer to something that happened even earlier. It establishes a sequence: event A happened, and before that, event B had already happened.

Formula: había/habías... + participio
yohabía hablado
túhabías hablado
él/ellahabía hablado
nosotroshabíamos hablado
vosotroshabíais hablado
elloshabían hablado
Timeline visualization
Earlier pastPluscuamperfecto
Later pastIndefinido
NowPresente
Key words: ya (already), todavía no (not yet), antes de que, cuando, nunca antes
⏮️
Action completed before another past action
Cuando llegué, ella ya había salido.
When I arrived, she had already left. (she left first, then I arrived)
😮
Reporting something someone hadn't done yet
Me dijo que nunca había visitado España.
He told me he had never visited Spain.
📖
In narratives — setting up backstory
No reconocí el lugar. Todo había cambiado.
I didn't recognise the place. Everything had changed.
Subjunctive · B2

Subjuntivo Imperfecto

B2

"If only... / I wished... / If I were..." — the B2 gateway tense

How to Form It

Take the ellos form of the Pretérito Indefinido, drop -ron, and add these endings: -ra, -ras, -ra, -ramos, -rais, -ran. Because it's built from the Indefinido, all those irregular stems carry over automatically — that's the trick!

hablar → hablaron → habla- + endings
yohablara
túhablaras
él/ellahablara
nosotroshabláramos
vosotroshablarais
elloshablaran
Irregulars inherit from Indefinido ellos
ser/ir (fueron)fuera, fueras…
tener (tuvieron)tuviera, tuvieras…
estar (estuvieron)estuviera…
hacer (hicieron)hiciera…
poder (pudieron)pudiera…
saber (supieron)supiera…
decir (dijeron)dijera…
querer (quisieron)quisiera…
🔀
If-clauses with unreal/hypothetical present situations (Si + Imp. Subj. → Conditional)
Si tuviera más tiempo, aprendería el piano.
If I had more time (but I don't), I would learn the piano.
⏪
Wishes/emotions/doubt expressed in the past (Sequence of Tenses)
Quería que vinieras a la reunión.
I wanted you to come to the meeting.
😔
Wishes about the present (Ojalá + Imp. Subj.)
Ojalá pudiera ir contigo. (but I can't)
I wish I could go with you.
🙏
Polite requests (quisiera = softer version of quiero)
Quisiera una mesa para dos, por favor.
I would like a table for two, please. (very polite)
💬
como si (as if) — always triggers Imp. Subj.
Habla como si supiera todo.
He talks as if he knew everything.
Key Structure · B1/B2

Si Clauses (If/Then)

Three patterns — each expressing a different level of likelihood

The Three Si Clause Patterns
Si clause (condition)
Result clause (consequence)
Si + Presente Indicativo Type 1 — real/likely condition (B1)
Si tienes tiempo...
→ Futuro / Imperativo / Presente If you have time... (and you might)
...llámame. / me llamas.
Si + Imperfecto Subjuntivo Type 2 — unreal/hypothetical present (B2)
Si tuviera dinero...
→ Condicional Simple If I had money... (but I don't)
...viajaría por el mundo.
Si + Pluscuamperfecto Subjuntivo Type 3 — impossible past condition (B2)
Si hubiera estudiado más...
→ Condicional Perfecto If I had studied more... (I didn't)
...habría aprobado el examen.
Never use Subjuntivo after si in if-clauses
"Si + Presente de Subjuntivo" is wrong. Si only takes Indicative (for likely) or Imperfecto/Pluscuamperfecto de Subjuntivo (for hypothetical). The exception: si meaning "whether" (like "I don't know if...") can take indicative only.
Future Perfect · B2

Futuro Perfecto

B2

"Will have done" — also used to speculate about the recent past

The Core Rule

Formed with haber (futuro) + participio. Use it to talk about an action that will be completed before a specific future moment. Also very useful for speculating about what "probably happened" in the recent past.

Formula: habré/habrás... + participio
yohabré hablado
túhabrás hablado
él/ellahabrá hablado
nosotroshabremos hablado
vosotroshabréis hablado
elloshabrán hablado
When to use
Future deadlinePara el viernes, habré terminado.
Before future eventCuando llegues, ya habré comido.
Past speculation¿Habrá llegado ya? (Has she probably arrived?)
Conditional Perfect · B2

Condicional Perfecto

B2

"Would have done" — the result of an impossible past condition

The Core Rule

Formed with haber (condicional) + participio. Almost always appears as the consequence of a Type 3 Si clause (Si + Pluscuamperfecto Subjuntivo → Condicional Perfecto). Also used for regret or criticism about the past.

Formula: habría/habrías... + participio
yohabría hablado
túhabrías hablado
él/ellahabría hablado
nosotroshabríamos hablado
vosotroshabríais hablado
elloshabrían hablado
Examples in context
Type 3 SiSi hubiera estudiado, habría aprobado.
RegretDebería haberlo dicho antes.
CriticismYo en tu lugar, no habría aceptado.
Reported speechDijo que lo habría hecho diferente.
Subjunctive · C1

Subjuntivo Pluscuamperfecto

C1

"Had done (in a subjunctive context)" — regret, impossible past wishes, and Type 3 si clauses

How to Form It

Formed with haber in the Imperfecto de Subjuntivo + participio: hubiera/hubieras/hubiera/hubiéramos/hubierais/hubieran + participio. This is the past version of the subjunctive — use it when a subjunctive trigger refers to something that already happened before the main clause.

Formula: hubiera + participio
yohubiera hablado
túhubieras hablado
él/ellahubiera hablado
nosotroshubiéramos hablado
vosotroshubierais hablado
elloshubieran hablado
Alternative -se form (equally valid)
yohubiese hablado
túhubieses hablado
él/ellahubiese hablado
nosotroshubiésemos hablado
vosotroshubieseis hablado
elloshubiesen hablado
😔

-ra vs -se forms
Both hubiera and hubiese are correct and interchangeable. The -ra form is more common in everyday speech and Latin America. The -se form is more literary/formal and common in Spain's written register. For interpreting, master both — you'll encounter both in professional contexts.
Advanced Structures · C1

Perífrasis Verbales

C1

Verb periphrases — the structures that make your Spanish sound native

What Are Perífrasis?

A perífrasis verbal is a combination of two verbs where the first verb is conjugated and adds meaning (obligation, duration, beginning, ending) to the second verb (infinitive or gerund). Mastering these is what separates B2 from C1 — they're extremely common in natural Spanish and critical for interpreting.

Interpreter Tip: deber vs deber de
Deber + infinitivo = obligation ("Debes estudiar" = You must study). Deber de + infinitivo = probability ("Debe de estar enfermo" = He must be sick). This distinction is fading in casual speech, but interpreters should maintain it — it's a mark of precision.

Literary Past · C2

Pretérito Anterior

C2

"As soon as he had done" — a rare literary tense you'll encounter in formal texts

The Core Rule

Formed with haber in the Pretérito Indefinido + participio. This tense is extremely rare in modern speech — it appears almost exclusively in literary and historical texts after conjunctions like cuando, en cuanto, apenas, tan pronto como, después de que. It expresses an action completed immediately before another past action.

Formula: hube/hubiste... + participio
yohube hablado
túhubiste hablado
él/ellahubo hablado
nosotroshubimos hablado
vosotroshubisteis hablado
elloshubieron hablado
Literary examples
ImmediacyApenas hubo terminado, se marchó.
SequenceEn cuanto hubo leído la carta, lloró.
Modern equiv.En cuanto terminó... (Indefinido replaces it)
Interpreter Note
You will almost never use this tense actively, but you must recognize it in literary, legal, and historical texts. In modern Spanish, it is replaced by the Pretérito Indefinido or the Pluscuamperfecto. If you encounter it while interpreting a formal text, render it naturally using "as soon as... had..."
Archaic / Legal · C2

Futuro de Subjuntivo

C2

"If it should be" — archaic tense alive in law, proverbs, and formal documents

How to Form It

Like the Imperfecto de Subjuntivo: take the ellos form of the Indefinido, drop -ron, and add: -re, -res, -re, -remos, -reis, -ren. This tense has disappeared from everyday speech but remains essential in legal and official Spanish — constitutions, contracts, and laws use it regularly.
