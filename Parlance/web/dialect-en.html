    Trap words

    Same word, different meaning — or socially risky.

    • pants — US: trousers. UK: often underwear. Prefer trousers in UK formal settings.
    • rubber — US: eraser sometimes; UK: condom. Prefer eraser / be explicit.
    • root / route — pronunciation and slang vary; AU “root” can be vulgar.
    • table a motion — US: postpone; UK: bring up for discussion. Clarify in meetings.
    • public school — US: state school; UK: fee-paying private school.

    Regional profiles

    At a glance for interpreters.

    • United States — rhotic accents common; -or/-ize spelling; healthcare/legal vocabulary often Latinate + insurance jargon.
    • United Kingdom — non-rhotic accents common; -our/-re spelling; NHS and court terminology differ from US.
    • Australia — often UK-aligned spelling; distinctive slang and intonation; “chips” ambiguity.
    • Canada — mix of US/UK spelling; vocabulary closer to US in speech; formal writing often UK-leaning.

    Don’t “correct” this

    In interpreting, convey meaning — don’t standardize the speaker’s variety.

    • UK colour / US color is not an error.
    • Australian slang is not “incorrect English.”
    • Canadian spelling mix is normal; don’t “fix” every -our to -or.
    • If the speaker is consistent in their variety, keep their lexical choices unless the listener truly won’t understand.
