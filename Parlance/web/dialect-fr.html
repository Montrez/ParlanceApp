    Pronouns and address

    The most practical difference between countries.

    Regiontu / vousQuick note
    France tu / vous Vous for formal, tu with people close to you. Plural: vous (formal and informal).
    Quebec tu / vous Tu is very common, even in semi-formal settings. Vous for real distance or respect.
    Belgium tu / vous Close to France, but numbers and some vocabulary differ (septante, nonante).
    French-speaking Switzerland tu / vous Like France/Belgium. Numbers: septante, huitante/octante, nonante.
    Africa tu / vous Vous often marks respect toward elders. Register is highly contextual.
    Caribbean tu / vous Strong Creole influence. Local rhythm and lexicon (Haiti, Antilles).

    Trap words

    Neutral in one country, problematic or misunderstood in another.

    blonde — In Quebec: girlfriend (f.). In France: blonde hair. Do not translate literally.
    char — In Quebec: car (casual). In France: tank (military) or old-fashioned tram.
    déjeuner / dîner — Meal times do not line up the same way in France vs Quebec. Clarify if it is an appointment.
    gosse — In France: kid (casual). In Quebec: can be vulgar (testicles). Avoid in formal contexts.
    sac — In France: handbag. In Quebec: can mean different things depending on context.
    jaser — Quebec: to chat. France: rarer; « bavarder » or « discuter » lands better.

    Regional profiles

    All six regions at a glance. If you only care about two, use the tool above.

    France

    Metropolitan France

    Address: tu widely with peers. Vocabulary: voiture, portable, mail, week-end, soixante-dix. Typography: space before ? ! ; :

    Québec

    Québec

    Address: tu very frequent. Vocabulary: char, courriel, fin de semaine, cellulaire, magasiner, blonde. Speech: chu (je suis), pis (et puis). Interpreting: standard Quebec French is not "bad French."

    Belgium

    Belgium

    Numbers: septante, nonante (not soixante-dix / quatre-vingt-dix). Vocabulary: une fois around Brussels can mean "please." Otherwise close to France.

    Switzerland

    French-speaking Switzerland

    Numbers: septante, huitante/octante, nonante. Vocabulary: natel (older word for cell phone), adieu (hello, not a final goodbye). Measured rhythm.

    Africa

    Francophone Africa

    Register: vous often marks respect. Lexicon: borrowings from local languages, vivid expressions. Interpreting: do not force Parisian French if the local variant is clear.

    Caribbean

    Francophone Caribbean (Haiti, Antilles)

    Contact: strong Creole influence. Rhythm: fast, with reductions. Interpreting: listen for false friends and cultural context before "correcting."

    Don't correct this

    In interpreting, you convey meaning. You do not homogenize.

    • Septante / nonante (BE, CH, QC) are not errors. They are standard regional French.
    • char, courriel, fin de semaine in Quebec are correct in a Quebec context.
    • Wider Quebec tutoiement is not impoliteness by default.
    • African or Creole borrowings are not "mistakes" if the speaker is consistent.
    • If the speaker is a native and clear, do not swap their word for yours unless meaning requires it.
