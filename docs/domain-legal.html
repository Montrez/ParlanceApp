Ethics

The five core canons

These come from the same professional standards judiciary interpreters are certified against (NAJIT, federal court interpreter canons). They are not optional style choices.

🎯
Accuracy & completeness
Interpret everything said, at the same register, without adding, omitting, editorializing, or softening. If a witness is vulgar or rambling, render that, not a cleaned-up version.
⚖️
Impartiality & conflict of interest
No advocacy for either side, no personal opinions on the case, and disclose any relationship to a party before proceeding. Decline the assignment if impartiality is compromised.
🔒
Confidentiality
Everything heard during an assignment, in the courtroom or in a private attorney-client conversation you're interpreting, stays confidential — including from other court staff.
🧭
Role boundaries
You are not an advocate, advisor, cultural broker, or paralegal. Don't explain legal concepts, offer opinions on strategy, or answer questions directed at you personally — redirect them through the judge/attorney.
🗣️
Professional demeanor & scope
Interpret only — don't give legal advice, don't fill out forms for a party, and disclose immediately if a passage was missed, unclear, or requires repetition.
Protocol

First person, and the three modes

Core rule

Always interpret in the first person as the speaker ('I understand the charges', not 'he says he understands'). Request clarification only through the judge, using a formula like 'the interpreter requests that counsel repeat the question.' Never paraphrase or summarize — especially not rights language.

ModeWhen it's used
ConsecutiveWitness testimony, attorney-client meetings — speaker pauses, you render the full segment.
SimultaneousWhispered/equipment-assisted rendering of proceedings for the defendant while others continue speaking (arraignments, trial narration).
Sight translationReading a written document aloud in the other language on the spot (a plea form, a police report) — read the whole document first, then translate.

Process

Stages of a criminal case

Knowing what happens at each stage helps you anticipate register and vocabulary before you're in the room.

1️⃣
Arrest & booking
Police take the person into custody, record charges, fingerprints, and photograph (mug shot).
2️⃣
Arraignment
Defendant is formally told the charges, enters a plea (guilty / not guilty / no contest), and bail/bond is set.
3️⃣
Preliminary hearing / grand jury
A judge (or grand jury) decides if there's enough evidence (probable cause) to proceed to trial.
4️⃣
Pretrial motions & plea bargaining
Attorneys file motions (e.g. to suppress evidence); many cases resolve here via a plea deal instead of trial.
5️⃣
Trial
Opening statements, testimony & cross-examination, closing arguments, jury instructions, verdict.
6️⃣
Sentencing & appeal
If convicted, the judge imposes a sentence at a separate hearing; either side may appeal to a higher court.
Rights

Miranda / caution language

Verbatim only
Rights advisories are fixed legal formulas. Render every clause, in order, with no summarizing — omitting even one clause can affect the case's outcome.
Standard Miranda warning, clause by clause
EN You have the right to remain silent.
ES Usted tiene el derecho a permanecer en silencio.
FR Vous avez le droit de garder le silence.
EN Anything you say can and will be used against you in a court of law.
ES Cualquier cosa que diga puede y será usada en su contra en un tribunal.
FR Tout ce que vous direz peut et sera utilisé contre vous devant un tribunal.
EN You have the right to an attorney.
ES Usted tiene el derecho a un abogado.
FR Vous avez le droit à un avocat.
EN If you cannot afford an attorney, one will be appointed for you.
ES Si no puede pagar un abogado, se le asignará uno.
FR Si vous n'avez pas les moyens d'engager un avocat, un avocat vous sera désigné.
  • Presumption of innocence — the defendant is presumed innocent until proven guilty beyond a reasonable doubt.
  • Render rights verbatim when the source is a fixed formula — do not shorten, even if the listener seems to already understand.
Traps

False cognates that change legal meaning

Looks like…
Actually means
sentencia looks like "sentence"
Correct here — but "sentencia" in everyday Spanish can also mean "ruling/judgment" generally, not only the punishment phase. Confirm which is meant.
record vs. récord
"Criminal record" ≠ "récord criminal" in the sports sense. Use "antecedentes penales" (ES) / "casier judiciaire" (FR).
carácter vs. character witness
"Testigo de carácter" is correct usage, but don't drift into "personalidad" — it's a specific legal term of art, not a general personality description.
constitutional vs. constitucional
Direct cognate is fine for "constitutional rights", but "constitucional" in medical Spanish also means "related to physical constitution" — context matters if a term crosses domains.
demand vs. demanda
"Demanda" in Spanish legal usage means "lawsuit/complaint", not "a demand/request". Don't render "demanda" as "demand" in a civil filing context.

Fixed phrases

Renditions to memorize verbatim

Oath
'Do you swear or affirm that the testimony you are about to give is the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth?' → ES: '¿Jura o afirma que el testimonio que está a punto de dar es la verdad, toda la verdad y nada más que la verdad?' → FR: « Jurez-vous ou affirmez-vous que le témoignage que vous allez donner est la vérité, toute la vérité, et rien que la vérité ? »
Verdict pronouncement
'We, the jury, find the defendant [guilty / not guilty] of the charge of ___.' Render the charge name exactly as given — do not substitute a colloquial equivalent.
